## What users get

The SLE line on the aging chart says which items have **already** missed the target. Nothing said
which ones are about to, so the window to act had closed by the time the problem was visible.

For a team that has published an SLE, three surfaces answer that:

- **A risk column** on every in-flight row of the work item dialog, sortable worst-first, reachable
  from a widget's View Data *or* from a click on any bubble on the Work Item Aging chart. Each cell
  discloses how much finished work its number rests on.
- **An At Risk widget** on Flow Overview — how many items are 70% or more likely to breach, with a
  status of its own derived from the target the team already published.
- **A write-back value source**, so the number reaches the board people actually work on.

The number is one conditional over the team's own history: *of every item that was still open at this
age, the share that went on to take longer than the target*. An item past the target reads 100% — not
by a rule, but because every item that ever ran that long necessarily ran longer than the target. It
is read over the throughput history the team configured and is always as of today, so it does not
move when a reader changes the date range — which is what lets the screens and the tracker agree.

### What round 2 changed, and why (2026-09-19)

| Round 1 said | Now | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Risk zones** as a third background mode on the aging chart | **Removed.** The control is Off / Pace percentiles again | The ladder answered a question the reference line already answered, and on a thin history it drew nothing at all in a mode the reader had explicitly chosen |
| **An at-risk count on the In Progress card**, at 50% — "more likely than not" | **A widget of its own**, at 70%, with a RAG status | One card cannot carry two meanings and a subtitle cannot have a status. Fifty counts most of a healthy board, and a count that is never small is never read |
| **A minimum-sample guard** refusing an answer below ten comparable items | **Removed.** Every in-flight item carries a number | The guard created a second silence that had to be told apart from the first everywhere, and the cure was worse: it blanked exactly the ages a coach cares about |
| **Two silences** — `Beyond history` and `Not enough history` | **Neither.** A thin history reads 0 and the cell says so in words | A sentinel in a numeric column is a value every consumer must special-case. The evidence now sits *beside* the answer instead of replacing it |
| The risk **follows the date range picker** | **It does not.** One window, one anchor, derived in one place | Two callers each deriving their own window is what produced a dialog reading 27% against a board field reading 18, for the same item on the same day |

## Decisions worth keeping

**The empirical conditional, not the declared one** (D1, [ADR-192](../product/architecture/adr-192-sle-risk-as-a-pure-conditional-over-the-cycle-time-population.md)).
The formula in the Epic description took its numerator from the *declared* SLE probability and its
denominator from the *empirical* distribution. Those agree only when the team is hitting its target
exactly. On a real-shaped 20-item history at 70% actual attainment, the declared form reads 67% at
day 9 where six of the seven items that ever got that far had breached. It also needs a clamp, a
divide-by-zero guard and a hard-coded certainty rule. The empirical form needs none of the three.

**The gate failed, and the answer was a guard rather than a stop.** — **REVERSED 2026-09-19.** The
guard is gone and every in-flight item carries a number; the instability it was built for is real,
and is now disclosed beside the answer rather than used to withhold one. The reasoning below is kept
because it is why the guard existed, and the reversal is argued in ADR-192's second amendment.
`OUT-4127-risk-stability` — slice
01's own learning hypothesis — was measured and came back negative. The displayed risk is a share of
`n(a)` comparable items, so one entering or leaving the window moves it by up to `100/n(a)` points;
on 602 real closed items the answer jumped 25 points overnight at the ages where the tail thins.
`SleRiskCalculator.MinimumComparableItems = 10` now refuses an answer below ten. The threshold is a
product judgement (ten caps the move at ten points and keeps the column populated; twenty would blank
most of the interesting ages).

**Two silences, never conflated** (D17). — **REVERSED 2026-09-19: there are now no silences at all.**
Both sentinels are gone, the DTO's comparable-item count went with them, and what replaced it is a
count of the team's finished work that was still open at this age — a fact about the history rather
than about the derivation, which is what lets one number be true beside every answer.
`Beyond history` means nothing ever ran this long;
`Not enough history` means too little did. Saying the first when nine items ran that long is a false
claim about the team's history, not a softer one. The DTO carries the comparable-item count so every
surface tells them apart, and the at-risk count treats the first as trouble and the second as
ignorance.

**The cache key had to carry the target, not just the window** (corrected in DELIVER). DDD-9 called
`SleRisk_{start}_{end}` complete on the grounds that ages are read as-of the range end — true, and
half the question. Nothing invalidates the metrics cache when a team's settings are saved, and every
other cached metric here is a function of stored work alone. Without the range in the key, a coach
tightening the target in one click keeps reading the old odds until the next refresh. Found by the
independent reviewer, not the author.

**Enum members are appended, because the ordinal is what is stored** (DDD-28). `ValueSource` persists
as an `int`. Inserting `SleRisk` in alphabetical order would silently re-point every existing
write-back mapping at a different source, on every customer's database, with no migration and no
error.

**No answer means no write** (DDD-29). A closed item, a team with no target, an item beyond all
history and an item too thinly evidenced all resolve to null and are absent from the write-back round
rather than clearing whatever the field held. The guard reaches the tracker for free — the one place
an unstable number would be worst, because Lighthouse cannot take it back.

## Corrected during delivery

**DISCUSS wrote slice 02 against a component that no longer ships.** `ItemsInProgress.tsx` — the card
whose `Goal: N` chip and fixed-width chip box AC-02.5 is entirely about — has no production caller;
only its own test imports it. Four of six acceptance criteria described something nobody sees. The
count went onto `WipOverviewWidget` instead and AC-02.5 was dropped rather than reinterpreted, because
the constraint it protected does not exist there. This is what the DESIGN wave is for; going straight
to DISTILL would have specified behaviour against dead code.

**The bands lied about what they did not know, and only a picture caught it.** The geometry painted
the last band to the top of the axis. When the higher bands are missing *because the evidence ran
out* — the normal case — that painted the calmest colour across every age nothing is known about. The
docs written one slice earlier promised the opposite in as many words. Every geometry test supplied a
full set of four bands, where the top one *is* certainty and running to the axis top is correct; the
defect only appears when a band is missing, and no test drew that chart. Each band now carries its
own upper edge, and only certainty is open-ended.

**The demo SLE went onto the wrong object first.** `CreateDemoProject` and `CreateDemoTeam` end with
the same two lines, so an edit anchored on them landed on the portfolio. The E2E failed with "the risk
mode never appeared", which pointed nowhere, and two server restarts were spent on a stale-binary
theory before the factory was tested directly. `DemoDataFactoryTest` now pins both values and would
have said so in a second. A shared closing fragment is a bad anchor; the test is the check.

**"Demo data — N/A, because the demo teams already carry an SLE" was asserted in three checklists and
was false.** No demo team published a target, so nothing in this Epic was visible on demo data at all.
Found while taking the screenshot, which could not be taken without discovering it. The demo teams now
ship 85% @ 7 days, on the maintainer's call — the demo is how people evaluate Lighthouse, and a
feature nobody can see does not get evaluated.

The runs earned their keep four times over: a numerator boundary at an age past the target; a cache
key no test distinguished; a null guard whose test proved nothing because LINQ throws the same
exception one line later; and a chart width computed as `right + left`, which passed only because
every fixture used a left edge of zero, and the chart's left edge never is.

One finding is worth remembering beyond this Epic: **a mutant that stops the tests running is
reported as survived.** A descriptor built at `describe` scope turns any throw into a collection
error, the file runs zero tests, and StrykerJS reads zero failures as survival. Per-survivor reasoning
is in `epic-4127-sle-risk/mutation-results.md`.
