## Provenance of the Elastic text

`elv2-verbatim.txt` was fetched from `elastic/elasticsearch` at
`licenses/ELASTIC-LICENSE-2.0.txt` and cross-checked byte-for-byte against
`elastic/kibana`'s copy of the same path. Both hash to:

```
sha256  48255018b41fc0e965b1115af7e6779bc218bb8a6747d561da800d5022622aa2
93 lines, 3860 bytes
```

**Regenerate and verify:**

```sh
gh api repos/elastic/elasticsearch/contents/licenses/ELASTIC-LICENSE-2.0.txt \
  --jq .content | base64 -d | sha256sum
cat license-header.txt elv2-verbatim.txt > LICENSE.draft
```

**Check the shipped LICENSE still carries Elastic's text unaltered** — this is
AC-01.1, and it must hold after every future edit to the header:

```sh
awk '/^={80}$/{n++; next} n==2' LICENSE | tail -n +2 | diff - elv2-verbatim.txt
```

## Drafting provenance, recorded here and deliberately not in the shipped files

Section 1 of the Additional Terms is derived from the Noncompete and Competition
clauses of **PolyForm Shield 1.0.0**, which is the one licence family whose
steward grants permission to adapt its text. That permission is conditional:
*"If you make changes to a PolyForm license, you must remove all mention of
'PolyForm' and polyformproject.org, as well."* The shipped document therefore
carries no such mention, and this note is why the attribution lives here instead.
`grep -ri polyform` over the shipped files must return nothing.

Two deliberate departures from Shield's wording:

- **"and even when they are provided only within a single organization"** is
  ours, not Shield's. Shield says competitors compete *"even when provided free
  of charge"*, which reaches a free external product but reads ambiguously
  against a purely internal rebuild. The internal rebuild is the case this epic
  exists for, so it is named.
- Shield's **Discontinued Products** and **Sales of Business** clauses are
  dropped. Neither applies to a two-person company with one product, and every
  clause that ships is a clause somebody has to read.

Section 2 has no precedent to derive from — no mainstream software licence
carries an AI clause. It is drafted here for the first time and is the part most
in need of the lawyer's eye.
